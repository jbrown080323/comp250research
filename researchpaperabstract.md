Within the past couple of years, the privacy of the internet has become an hot topic, especially in area's such as social media, where teenagers and younger are most affected. As a result, this has led to discussions on how to adress it to protect the many users of the internet, which have steadly intensified with every passing year. 

However, the method to do so has been heavily debated, with the current policies set in place have been critized by many people against it. Among those critizied are social media corporations and the government itself, both who have been questioned on if they can be trusted with the data of millions of users, as well as how secure it would be if they were managing the data, given the existence of hackers and now, the rise of artifical intelligence and the worries of what data the AI models hold only add more questions to the already divisive issue. On the other hand, the lack of action would only let the issue of online safety grow worse overtime, so some believe that the laws should be strict for the safety ofeveryone on the internet, especally for the most vulnerable.

What this paper aims to do is understand how safe users of the Internet are with in the 21st century, what should be done to protect users, and whether, if at all theere should be stricker laws placed.
