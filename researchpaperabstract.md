Within the past couple of years, the privacy of the internet has become an hot topic, especially in area's such as social media, where teenagers and younger are most affected. As a result, this has led to discussions on how to adress it to protect the many users of the internet, which have steadily intensified with every passing year. 

However, the method to do so has been heavily debated, with the current policies set in place have been criticized by many people against it. 
Social media corporations and the government itself have been questioned and criticized by many on whether if they can be trusted with the data of millions of usersand how secure it would be if they were managing the data, given the existence of hackers and now, the rise of artifical intelligence and the worries of what data the A.I models hold only add more questions to the already divisive issue. 
On the other hand, the lack of action would only let the issue of online safety grow worse overtime, so some believe that the laws should be strict for the protection of everyone on the internet, especially for the most vulnerable.

What this paper aims to do is understand how safe users of the Internet are with in the 21st century, what should be done to protect users, and whether, if at all there should be stricker laws placed.
